Notes on the fretboard

Filled dots are natural notes (A to G). Hollow dots are sharps or flats. Fret 0 is the open string. Top string is the highest, bottom is low E.

How to figure it out
  • The musical alphabet is A B C D E F G, then it starts again. Most letters have a sharp after them: A#, C#, D#, F#, G#. There is no sharp between B and C or between E and F, so those pairs are one fret apart.
  • A flat is the same fret as a sharp, named from the note above: A# = Bb, C# = Db, D# = Eb, F# = Gb, G# = Ab. Same spot, two names.
  • Going up a string, each fret is the next note in that sequence. Fret 12 is the open note again, one octave higher.
  • Neighbouring strings are 5 frets apart: fret 5 on a string matches the open string above it. The exception is G to B, which is 4.
  • Octave shape: from any note, go two strings up and two frets along. If that crosses the G to B gap, go three frets.
  • Bass is the bottom four guitar strings (E A D G) an octave lower, so everything here applies to it too. Learn the low E and A strings first, since most roots live there.
Scale patterns

Coral dots are the root. The shaded band is the pattern's fret range. Top string is the highest, bottom is low E.

How to figure it out
  • The five patterns are fixed shapes. They look the same in every key, and only their starting fret changes.
  • Find your root on the low E string (the info line shows its fret). That decides where each pattern sits on the neck.
  • Patterns 1 to 5 always come in the same order. Each one overlaps the next, so together they cover the whole neck, then pattern 1 repeats 12 frets higher.
  • A minor scale shares its notes with the major scale whose root is 3 frets higher (A minor = C major). Minor pentatonic does the same with major pentatonic, so one set of shapes has two names.
  • On bass the shapes are the same on its four strings.
